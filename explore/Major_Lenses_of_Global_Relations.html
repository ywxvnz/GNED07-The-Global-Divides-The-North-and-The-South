Realism

Realists believe that the international system is uneven, conflictual, and marked by power struggle. States prioritize self-interest and survival, and the global North interacts with the global South in order to promote their very own interest.

Liberalism / Constructivism

Liberals oppose realism because of a more optimistic vision in the international system. They believe that balance, harmony, cooperation, mutual respect, trade, and economic interdependence make war less likely to happen.

Constructivists also convey cooperation, trust, and peace among international actors, but these are possible only through existing norms, conduct, and institutions that promote peace in the international system.

Marxism

Marxism highlights the structures of economic power and inequalities in the global system. It argues that capitalist or industrialized countries in the global North dominate and exploit the global South, resulting in unequal benefits and an uneven economic structure.

Postmodernism debunks ideas of hierarchy, dogmas, and existing structures in global relations. It believes that truth is always contested and plural, and that language and discourse can disclose or illustrate power relations.
