Third World versus Global South

The global South is not a directional designation but a symbolic designation connected to former colonial entities that engaged in decolonization and moved toward a postcolonial international order.

States in the global South share common experiences such as colonization and solidarity against colonialism. After WWII and the creation of the UN in 1945, many ex-colonies achieved independence, leading to the emergence of the First (capitalist), Second (communist), and Third (non-aligned) worlds. The Bandung Conference in 1955 showed the solidarity of Asian and African countries against colonialism and emphasized economic independence, well-being, freedom, and universal human rights.
